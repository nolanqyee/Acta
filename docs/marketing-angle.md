# Acta — Marketing Angle

Last updated: 2026-08-04

**Purpose:** Positioning and messaging SoT for landing copy, pitch, and GTM. Describes the **planned product** (see [`personal-evidence-graph.md`](personal-evidence-graph.md)). Not pixels on screen today.

**Distilled idea:**

> Most experiences lose value because we forget them. Acta makes every experience more valuable over time.

---

## What Acta actually is (for marketing)

Acta is not primarily an AI résumé builder, a graph database, a second brain, or another notes app.

It is a system for **preserving and compounding the value of your own life**.

The graph, AI extraction, résumé generation, and interview answers are **how** that value is delivered. They are not the sell.

---

## The core insight

People don't lose opportunities because they lack experiences.

They lose opportunities because they **forget** them.

The project built sophomore year. The metric measured. The conversation that changed how you think. The bug that took two days. The leadership moment that would have made a perfect interview story.

None of those disappear because they weren't important. They disappear because memory fades.

Acta preserves those moments while they're still fresh, then reconnects them when they become relevant months or years later.

**That is the product.**

---

## The pain (freshman at UT)

Every week: CS assignments, hackathons, student orgs, coffee chats, side projects, clubs, classes, random ideas, internship applications.

Every application asks: *Tell me about yourself.*

Every time: *Wait... what have I actually done?*

The weird thing is you **did** do it. You just don't remember it anymore.

The pain is not writing. Not résumés.

**Memory.**

## The mental model

Every experience has hidden future value.

The problem: you don't know **which** experience, or **when** it will matter.

- A random course project might become your strongest interview story.
- A club event might answer a scholarship essay.
- A bug fixed today might explain your engineering philosophy three years from now.

The only winning strategy: **preserve everything while it's fresh.** Acta figures out what matters later.

When you're 18, you don't know what interview stories you'll need, what essays you'll write, what startup you'll build, or what values you'll care about. So the optimal strategy is save everything, then let relevance sorting happen when the ask arrives. That is much stronger than "build a graph."

---

## Compound memory

Everyone understands compound interest. Nobody talks about **compound memory**.

Most experiences lose value over time. Not because they become less meaningful. Because you forget the details.

Acta reverses that. Every new experience adds context to the ones before it:

- Your internship makes your coursework more meaningful.
- Your side project reframes what you learned during your internship.
- Your leadership role changes how you talk about an old hackathon.

Nothing exists in isolation anymore. Your experiences **compound** instead of fading.

**Example:**

| Year | What you remember without Acta | What Acta surfaces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freshman | "I made a website." | Same, plus links |
| Sophomore | "I built an internal tool." | Connects to freshman work |
| Junior | "I optimized Redis." | Backend thread emerging |
| Senior, asked about backend systems | Only Redis | "Wait... every project I've done has actually been backend." |

The graph didn't create the narrative. It **uncovered** it.

**Killer word:** compound.

---

## Why the graph exists (internal framing)

Users do not care about having a graph. Users care that their **life is connected**.

The graph makes those connections visible. Instead of one résumé bullet, you see how one project led to another, how one class inspired a side project, how one conversation changed your direction.

The graph preserves relationships that memory destroys.

Example branch (not one accomplishment, one branch of your life):

```
Discord bot
  HackTX
  Freshman fall
  Learned Node
  Met Jason
  Used Redis
  Inspired Bubble feature
  Led to internship interview story
  Taught a teammate
```

**Say:** "Watch connections appear between experiences you thought were unrelated."

**Don't say:** "Build a graph."

---

## Relevance over time (why résumé framing is too small)

Record today:

> TA asked me to explain Dijkstra's algorithm to another student.

Seems meaningless. Then:

| When | Ask | That memory |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eight months later | Google: tell me about mentoring | Matters |
| Two years later | Applying to be a TA | Matters again |
| Three years later | Manager interview | Matters again |
| Five years later | Teaching statement | Matters again |

The event never changed. Its **relevance** did. Résumés cannot represent that. Acta can.

---

## The emotional promise

When you look back, you don't remember why you built things, what you struggled with, how you thought, the conversations, the tiny wins. Those are just gone.

Acta preserves a younger version of yourself. That is more emotional than résumés.

**Lines:**

- Your memories fade. Your growth shouldn't.
- Acta keeps the details alive, so years from now you can still remember not just what you accomplished, but how you got there.
- Every note is a gift to future you. Future-you doesn't remember today's details. Acta does.

## Hero alternatives (exploration)

Framework for picking a hero: **h1 owns one angle**; **sub owns memory pain + payoff**; **eyebrow is the hook or category**. Animation works when line 2 completes a grammatical clause, not when a lone verb floats.

**Cycle word sets by theme:**

| Theme | Verbs to cycle |
| --- | --- |
| Compounding | compounds · builds · adds up |
| Memory / resurface | resurfaces · comes back · stays |
| Connection | connects · links · fits together |
| Value over time | grows · matters · lasts |

Current cycle (`compounds · resurfaces · connects`) mixes all three. Consider **one theme per hero** so the animation reinforces a single idea.

### A. Compounding (company bet)

**A1 — Current shape (refined)**

```
Everything you
do, and how it
compounds / resurfaces / connects.
```

*Everything you do, and how it compounds.*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Pros | Grammatically sound; familiar 3-line rhythm; "how it" clause works |
| Cons | "Everything you do" is abstract; mixes three themes in the cycle; second clause can feel like mechanism not emotion |

**A2 — Compound only (single-theme cycle)**

```
Everything you
do, and how it
compounds / builds / adds up.
```

Tighter animation. All compounding language.

**A3 — Direct subject-verb (2 lines, no "how it")**

```
Everything you do
compounds / resurfaces / connects.
```

Grammatically valid (*Everything you do compounds*). Risk: line 2 is only the highlighted verb unless you keep `and how it` on line 2 as prefix in smaller weight... Actually line 2 would be full "compounds." with highlight. Pair with a **longer line 1** or **3-line break**:

```
Everything
you do compounds.
```
(Awkward break)

Better as **inline one-liner** at smaller breakpoints:

```
Everything you do compounds.
```
(cycle word highlighted inline; may wrap as one sentence on desktop)

**A4 — "Compound memory" explicit**

```
Your experiences
compound / connect / resurface
over time.
```

Static tail "over time" gives line 2 mass. Slightly corporate ("experiences").

### F. Hybrid layouts (h1 + eyebrow swap)

Sometimes the **emotional hook moves up** so the h1 can stay simple:

| Eyebrow | h1 |
| --- | --- |
| You do more than you'll remember | Nothing you do disappears |
| Waitlist open · remember everything | Everything you do, and how it compounds |
| Every experience has future value | Capture once. Benefit forever. |

This avoids h1 grammar contortions and keeps memory pain out of a challenging headline.

### Recommendation matrix

| If you prioritize… | Try first | Animation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strongest single line, least fuss | **B1** Nothing you do disappears | Static |
| Compound memory + motion | **A2** compound-only cycle with "how it" | Yes |
| Application pain | **C2** or **C3** | C2 yes, C3 static |
| Emotional / future self | **D2** Future you won't remember today | Static |

**Open question:** Is hero animation earning its complexity? The strongest lines in this doc (**Nothing you do disappears**, **Future you won't remember today**) are static. Animation may fit compounding best; memory angles may not need it.

## Messaging principles

| Don't sell | Sell |
| --- | --- |
| AI | Remembering |
| A graph | Connected experiences |
| Résumés | Never starting from scratch |
| Outputs | Preserving the experiences that make those outputs possible |

Users don't wake up wanting "one connected record." They wake up wanting:

- "I wish I remembered everything I've done."
- "I hate rewriting my life every application."
- "I know I've done more than I can remember."
- "I wish all my experiences connected together."

The graph is the mechanism. Memory, compounding, and never starting from scratch are the outcomes.

## Audience and wedge (GTM context)

**First wedge:** college students recruiting for tech internships. Memory pain is acute: high volume of experiences, high frequency of "tell me about yourself," low retention of detail.

**Long-term:** same record grows across life stages. Messaging can soften from "internship" to "opportunity" as adapters expand. Do not promise essay-writing product.

**"Not impressive enough":** you can extract more value from your experiences than you think. Acta does not require feeling accomplished to start.
